# Duel test — The Funniest Toaster Error Message

Competition: `u-eager-stork-81-9647-the-funniest-toaster-error-436406cd`
($2, winner-takes-all, 1 paid place; closes 2026-11-26T05:10Z).

## Method

Proxy judge: me, applying the kit brief verbatim (competition.yaml /
RULES.txt), criteria in order:

1. Is a real error message a kitchen toaster would show its owner, and
   the funniest of its kind — anything else loses to any funny toaster
   error message.
2. Freshness — the surprise arrives from somewhere the obvious toaster
   puns never go.
3. Tightness — every word earns its place, as short as it can be rather
   than as long as it may be.
4. One clear voice for a small appliance speaking to its owner, held to
   the end.
5. Lands on a single read, no setup needing a second sentence.

Each pair was judged in BOTH orders (A-vs-B and B-vs-A), margin 1–5,
no ties, exactly like the real judge's game scoring: each call gives the
picked entry 0.5 + margin/10 (0.6 at margin 1 up to 1.0 at margin 5) and
the other the rest; a game's score is the mean of its two calls.

The kit's 3 house anchor texts are sealed (not in the kit), so no duel
against anchors was possible; the bracket below is internal, 10 drafts,
full round-robin of 8 plus 2 late challengers against the top 3.

Note on length: this competition's own brief says the line should be
"as short as it can be rather than as long as it may be", and the
tie-break goes to the smaller text (fewer bytes) before earlier receipt.
So short was treated as an advantage here, not a failure to use budget.
